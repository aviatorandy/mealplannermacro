What the research says

0.7-1.0 g per lb is the top of the studied range.

That equals 1.6-2.2 g/kg. A meta-analysis of 49 trials found muscle gains from extra protein leveled off around 0.73 g/lb (1.6 g/kg) in people lifting weights. Going to 1 g/lb adds a safety margin while dieting.

Morton et al., Br J Sports Med 2018

With a lot of weight to lose, use goal weight.

Per-pound formulas based on total weight can overstate needs when body fat is high. Clinical reviews suggest goal, adjusted or lean weight instead. Enter your goal weight under Goals to see that range.

Protein requirement in obesity (review)

Higher protein helps keep muscle while losing fat.

Trials of calorie-cut diets found more fat loss and better muscle retention with more protein, mostly among people who stuck with it. 200 g protein is 800 kcal, so lean sources matter.

Leidy et al., Am J Clin Nutr 2015

Total calories still decide weight loss.

A meta-analysis of 13 trials found adding breakfast did not help weight loss. Spreading protein across meals helps with fullness, but the daily calorie number is what moves the scale.

Sievert et al., BMJ 2019

Planning tool, not medical advice. High protein intake is generally considered safe for healthy kidneys; if you have kidney disease, diabetes or take diet-sensitive medication, check your targets with a doctor or registered dietitian.
