Why these targets

What the research supports, and where it does not.

Aim for 1.2-1.6 g protein per kg body weight while losing weight.

Controlled trials found higher-protein, calorie-reduced diets led to more fat loss and better muscle retention than standard-protein diets. Benefits showed up mainly in people who stuck with it.

Leidy et al., Am J Clin Nutr 2015

About 25-35 g protein at breakfast is a practical target.

In a small study, a 35 g protein breakfast increased fullness and cut evening snacking compared with a cereal breakfast of the same calories. Plain Greek yogurt gives about 17 g per 170 g cup for 100 calories.

Leidy, Univ. of Missouri 2013 (20 participants)

Eating breakfast does not cause weight loss by itself.

A meta-analysis of 13 trials found breakfast eaters ate about 260 more calories a day and weighed slightly more than skippers. Total daily calories decide weight loss; a protein breakfast helps only if it keeps you full and on target later.

Sievert et al., BMJ 2019

Plain beats flavored yogurt, and fiber matters too.

Flavored yogurts often carry 9+ g added sugar per cup. US guidelines cap added sugar at 10% of calories and suggest 14 g fiber per 1,000 calories. Berries, chia and flax add fiber without much sugar.

Dietary Guidelines for Americans
